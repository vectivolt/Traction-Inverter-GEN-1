# Marine Series — propulsion inverter cells (M8 · M10)

A **separate product series** from the Road GEN-1 traction inverter. It has its own product
codes, ratings, firmware build, enclosure, type approval and change control. It shares the
Road design's supply chain, verification method and, for M8, a frozen fork of its PCBs.
Status 2026-09-23: **definition and verified sizing — no marine hardware is built.**

- Engineering basis, sources and decisions: [`design-basis.md`](design-basis.md)
- Every number below: [`verification-report.md`](verification-report.md), generated by
  `npm run marine` (**86 PASS · 22 WARN · 0 FAIL**; each WARN names a vendor, class or bench gate)

## The idea in one paragraph

A marine propulsion drive is sized by **continuous** current, not a 30 s peak. It must
reduce power instead of tripping, and it must survive a single failure. So the product is a
**3-phase cell** (≈150–250 kW continuous), and power is built up from **cells per motor**.
A MW-class PM propulsion motor is built as **N separate 3-phase winding sets**
(6/9/12-phase = dual/triple/quad), and each set is fed by its own cell. Danfoss (DUAL/QUAD
EM-PMI) and The Switch (dual-winding MW machines) already sell such motors. Losing a cell
costs 1/N of the torque, not the ship. "Low RPM, high torque" is the motor's job. The inverter
sees an ordinary 40–80 Hz output, because low-speed PM motors have many poles.

Rating basis:

- 45 °C coolant.
- Tj ≤ 125 °C continuous, with a 110 % / 60 s overload.
- Life ≥ 2 × a 20-year mission (LESIT model).

\*Tug duty assumes 6 jobs × 20 full-power pulses per day. The pulse count is an estimate: an
owner's measured profile moves this rating, not the hardware.

**Why IGBT for marine, and when SiC.**

- **1200 V IGBT for 800 V-class buses.** Cosmic-ray failures stay at about 1–7 FIT per switch
  up to 850 V; that is why M8 stops there.
- **1700 V IGBT for the 1000 V DC grids** that ferries and tugs actually use. Their batteries
  reach 1.1–1.2 kV at full charge; Siemens and Danfoss use 1700–1800 V parts there.
- **SiC only for small craft,** where +0.3 pt efficiency is range. It is limited by:
  - motor-cable reflections (4 m of cable nearly doubles the terminal voltage);
  - a worse power-cycling factor (0.33×);
  - no hiitio short-circuit or cosmic-ray data yet.
- **Not used:** 3300 V modules (medium voltage, beyond these LV DC grids).

## Battery / BMS voltage windows

Pack series count that keeps the whole state of charge inside the cell's window:

|---|---|---|
| LFP 2.5 / 3.2 / 3.65 | **211–225 s** (225 s: 563 / 720 / 821 V) | **274–292 s** (292 s: 730 / 934 / 1066 V) |
| NMC 3.0 / 3.65 / 4.2 | **176–196 s** (196 s: 588 / 715 / 823 V) | **229–254 s** (254 s: 762 / 927 / 1067 V) |
| LTO 1.5 / 2.3 / 2.7 | 305 s: derates in the last few % SOC | 395 s: derates below 650 V |
| Genset DC bus | 400 / 440 V gensets (540–653 V) | 690 V gensets (932–1025 V) |

The BMS must:

- hold charging at ≤ 97 % of the cell's V_max;
- send charge and discharge current limits on CAN (the cell caps regeneration at them);
- report insulation (IT network).

Your "2–3 MW" guess for tugs is low. The sourced tugs show about 1.55–1.8 t of bollard pull per
100 kW, so a 60–70 t tug is about **2 × 2 MW (≈ 4 MW)**.

## Do MW motors need a starter? No.

The inverter is the soft starter. It applies controlled current from 0 Hz, with no 6–8×
direct-on-line inrush, and a PM rotor needs no magnetising time. The real MW-level items are
handled in firmware or a small kit, and are verified:

| Item | How it's handled |
|---|---|
| Full torque at 0 rpm (fouled propeller, pushing tug) | switching drops to 1 kHz below 2 Hz; M8 holds 134 % and M10 110 % indefinitely |
| Propeller already turning | flying start from the resolver |
| Rejoining a live DC bus | per-cell precharge; without it the inrush is ≈ 5–6 kA |
| Voltage from the motor when the drive is off | back-EMF rule in the motor specification (E_LL,pk at 115 % overspeed ≤ 836 / 1093 V) keeps the rectified voltage under the trip at every speed. The winding's stored energy counts too (Road rev A.11, applied at every speed since round 13/A.12): the cell's own link holds it only to ≈ 105 / 108 A for a motor built to the 1 pu short-circuit-current rule, so switching off relies on the DC grid staying connected, and the cell short-circuits the motor (ASC) if the DC path opens |
| Genset buses and crash stops | power-management ramps and fast load reduction; regeneration capped at the BMS limit |

## Regenerative braking — who owns it

The cell executes it; the ship's systems decide it.

**Decide, and set the limits:**
- the propulsion control sets how hard to brake;
- the BMS sets how much charge the battery can take;
- the power-management system sets the power ramps.

**Execute, and protect (the cell):**
- runs negative torque within those limits;
- trips overvoltage when a battery breaker opens mid-braking: ≤ 15.6 µs to the ASC request plus ≤ 7.56 µs
  ASC entry (link peak 909 / 1178 V);
- carries the regeneration current in the diodes at 96–97 °C.

On battery vessels the battery is the brake. A genset-only bus needs a brake chopper: that is a
standard cell in chopper mode with external resistors (≈260–300 kW per cell), so no new hardware.

## What changes from the Road series

**M8** keeps the Road PCBs, frozen at **rev A.12** (review rounds 7–13: safety logic, ASC, gate
supply, bias binding, LV feed switch, SPO energy rule (now applied at every speed), resolver,
V_DC bias, Hall interface, LV-entry TVS, barrier-part orderability, MCU pin freeze; list in
design-basis §9). It adds:

- the marine kit, about ₹27–56k per cell:
  - IP54 enclosure;
  - anti-condensation heater;
  - isolated 24 → 12 V control supply, fed from class-grade battery-backed 24 V, because a towed PM
    propeller can need the active short held for hours. The card's LV entry is a 12 V design. Its TVS
    (TPSMC24CA-VR since A.11) is dark at 24 V, but ship 24 V reaches 31.2 V, above its 26.7–29.5 V
    breakdown. The power board's V15 rail is also a boost, which cannot step 24 V down: its
    post-regulator would then run into thermal shutdown and take the ASC and V_DC bias with it;
  - DC fuses and precharge;
  - leak sensor;
- a marine identity resistor (47 k), so Road firmware refuses marine hardware and vice versa;
- the MFW-01…16 firmware (multi-set control, 6ω harmonic loops, power reduction instead of trip,
  BMS/PMS contracts, standstill mode, optional brake-chopper mode);
- IRS/DNV type approval;
- at system level, for multi-set motors: a per-set motor disconnector that opens on a cell
  fault. A shorted switch must not leave an asymmetric short while the other sets keep the
  shaft turning.

**M10** adds a new 1100 V power stage:

- 1700 V modules;
- 20 × 1300 V-class cans;
- 3 DESAT diodes;
- a certified flyback transformer (≥ 1150 V DC working, gate ⑤) — the bias module, opto and
  Y-caps are now the same Road A.12 parts as M8 (UCC14141-Q1, VOW3120-X017T,
  VY1472M63Y5UQ6TV0), already rated ≥ 1150 V DC;
- re-rated discharge (2 × 8 × 22 k bleeder, 5 × 470 Ω active) and V_DC divider (8 resistors).

Three Road-series findings came out of this work. They are fixed on the Road side and logged as
N7–N9 in [`../docs/review-A6-disposition.md`](../docs/review-A6-disposition.md):

- **N7:** the LEM HC5FW is a reduced-insulation part, so the busbar needs a sleeve.
- **N8:** TLP152 had no working-voltage rating; order the VDE (V4) option — since closed, below.
- **N9:** the overvoltage reaction (FW-06) was budgeted at 100 kW of regeneration; at full regen
  with the battery path lost it must be fast (firmware only). Since round 7 that is a measured
  chain: ≤ 15.6 µs to the ASC request, then ≤ 7.56 µs ASC entry.

N7 is a row of the Road barrier register ([`../docs/design-basis.md`](../docs/design-basis.md)
§6a, gate ⑩) and stays open (busbar sleeve + LEM sign-off). N8 and the QA01C-18/Y-cap items
(gates ⑪ and ㉔, extended at round 12) are **closed at BOM level by round 13**: the ASC/discharge
optos are now Vishay **VOW3120-X017T** (V_IORM 1414 Vpk) and their bias modules TI
**UCC14141-Q1** (V_IORM 1414 Vpk, V_IOWM 1000 Vrms / 1414 V DC), with the Y-caps bound to Vishay
**VY1472M63Y5UQ6TV0** (1500 V DC). In the Marine barrier register
(`marine/calc/marine-verify.mjs`), M8 (850 V) now reads **PASS** on the opto, bias and Y-cap rows
with these parts. M10 (1150 V working) is covered by the *same* parts (1414 Vpk / 1414 V DC /
1500 V DC, all ≥ 1150 V) — its "certified bias module / SO6L-class opto" asks are satisfied too,
and its Y1/500 VAC Y-cap class (sized for the 690 V AC grid case, not the DC bus) is not the
limiting figure. The only residual is the certificate status: VDE/UL/CQC are listed "planned" in
the UCC14141-Q1 datasheet, so check them at PO. The flyback transformer (gate ⑤) is still open for
M10.

## Cost and roadmap

These are planning numbers from the Road cost-rollup method, RFQ ±25 %. They exclude the
one-time type approval (₹40–80 L).

| | Ex-works per cell | ₹ per continuous kW |
|---|---|---|
| M8 | ≈ ₹1.2–1.5 L | ≈ 560–700 |
| M10 | ≈ ₹1.4–1.7 L | ≈ 550–670 |

1. **Phase 1 — M8.** No new PCB: kit, firmware and type approval. Serves water taxis,
   inland/coastal ferries and water-metro craft.
2. **Phase 2 — M10.** Serves ferries and 32–45 t tugs on 1000 V grids.
3. **Phase 3 — M10-2P.** Built on a committed 60–70 t tug programme (GTTP: 16 tugs by 2027,
   50 by 2030).
4. **In parallel:** a motor partner for DUAL/QUAD PM motors, one resolver per set.

The open gates (hiitio, Faratronic, certified barrier parts, class texts, motor partner, owner
duty profiles, bench tests) are listed in design-basis §12.
